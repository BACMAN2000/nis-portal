    📖 Writing guide

    The writing process
    1 Brainstorm - note every idea (who? what? why?). 2 Plan - put ideas in order, one per paragraph. 3 Draft - write freely, fix it later. 4 Revise - make each idea clear, add an example, cut repeats. 5 Proofread - hunt for mistakes and read it aloud.
    Build a strong paragraph (P.E.E.L.)
    P - Point: one sentence with the main idea.
    E - Evidence: a fact, example or quote.
    E - Explain: say how it proves your point.
    L - Link: connect to the next idea.
    Example: "Sport is good for teenagers. For example, it lowers stress. This is because exercise releases endorphins. Therefore, schools should offer more sport."
    Write a thesis statement
    A thesis is one sentence that states your main argument, usually at the end of your introduction.
    Formula: Topic + your position + main reason.
    Example: "Schools should teach coding because it builds problem-solving and future job skills." Make it specific and arguable.

    Proofread like a pro
    Read your work aloud. Check one thing at a time: CAPITAL letters, end punctuation (. ? !), spelling and verb tense. Make sure each paragraph has one main idea and uses linking words. Leave it for a few minutes, then read it again with fresh eyes.
    Academic styles cheat-sheet
    Descriptive - facts only, no opinion.
    Analytical - explain how & why; find patterns.
    Critical - weigh strengths and limits, then judge.
    Persuasive - take a position, give evidence, answer the other side.
    Reflective - describe an experience, your feelings, and what you learned.
