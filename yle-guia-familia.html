What the Young Learners exams are

These are Cambridge exams for children. In a friendly way, with activities similar to what happens in class, they measure how your child is doing in English. There is no pass or fail: every child receives a certificate with 12345 shields for each part of the exam, up to 15 in total.

Pre A1 StartersThe first contact. About 45 minutes in total. Vocabulary of around 460 words.
A1 MoversSimple sentences and short stories. About 1 hour. Adds around 370 new words.
A2 FlyersTells and writes short stories. About 1 h 15 min. Adds around 470 new words.

The three levels follow one another and lead into the secondary exams (A2 Key for Schools, B1 Preliminary for Schools). What your child learns at each level is used at the next one.

What the exam is like

It has three parts, called papers. Two are done in the classroom with the group (Listening and Reading & Writing) and one is a short one-to-one conversation with an examiner (Speaking).

The Speaking part is nothing to worry about. Before going in, a member of school staff explains to the child, in Spanish, what is going to happen and introduces them to the examiner. Inside there is only a friendly examiner who shows pictures and asks simple questions. If the child does not understand, the examiner repeats the question.

What to expect on exam day

Before

  • Make sure they sleep well the night before and have a calm breakfast.
  • Arrive with plenty of time: the school lets you know the time and the classroom. There is nothing special to bring.
  • Pencil, eraser and coloured pencils (colouring is needed in Listening); the school will have them anyway.
  • Tell them it is “like the practice tests done on the platform”: the format is the same.

During

  • Recordings are played twice; no one will rush them.
  • If they do not know an answer, they move on to the next one. Nothing takes points away.
  • In Speaking they can answer with one word or with “yes / no”: that already counts.
  • There is a break after each paper; Speaking is usually at another time on the same day, or on another date the school announces.

Afterwards

Results reach the school a few weeks after the exam, and the paper certificate arrives later. The certificate shows the shields for each part. Five shields in a part means the child did very well; one or two means they are still learning that skill, and that is normal.

How to read the school report

On the platform, each child practises with full tests that have the same format and timing as the real exam. With those results the school works out an estimated shield score so the family knows how things are going.

⚠️ The shields on the report are a school estimate based on the practice tests. Cambridge does not publish its conversion table, so the official result may be different. They are a guide, not a promise.

The report also says what the child can already do in each skill (using Cambridge’s official descriptions for their level) and what is worth practising at home.

How to help at home (without knowing English)

  • Five minutes a day are worth more than one hour on Sunday: a round of the vocabulary trainer or one part of a test.
  • Ask them to explain a picture in English, or to “teach” you three new words: explaining what they learned helps it stick.
  • Listen to the test audio together: hearing English with British and American accents is part of the exam.
  • Songs, cartoons and stories in English all count as Listening practice.
  • Celebrate the stars and the estimated shields; avoid comparing with other children.
